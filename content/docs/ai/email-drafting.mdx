## How drafting works

When you click **Draft with AI** in the email composer, the assistant reads the record you are writing against — the contact's details, recent communication, linked deal, last activity — and generates a draft in the tone you select.

You are never drafting from a blank page. The assistant has context, so the output references real details instead of generic placeholders.

## Drafting a follow-up

1. Open a contact, deal or ticket detail view
2. Click **Email** or open the composer
3. Click **Draft with AI**
4. Choose tone: warm professional, casual friendly, formal, or brief
5. Optionally add a one-line instruction ("mention the revised quote", "ask about the timeline")
6. Review the draft — edit, regenerate, or approve

<Callout variant="tip">
The more activity on the record, the better the draft. A contact with ten logged emails and three call notes produces a much more relevant follow-up than one you added yesterday.
</Callout>

## Tone control

| Tone | Best for |
|---|---|
| Warm professional | Most business correspondence |
| Casual friendly | Established relationships, internal comms |
| Formal | Enterprise buyers, legal, procurement |
| Brief | Quick check-ins, confirmations |

The tone applies to vocabulary, sentence length and sign-off style. It does not change the substance.

## What is sent to the AI provider

Only the specific context needed for the draft:

- The contact's name, company and role
- Recent email subjects and snippets (not full threads)
- The deal stage and value (if linked)
- Your instruction (if provided)

Your full database is never sent. Every request is scoped to the record you are drafting against.

## Credit cost

A single draft typically costs 1–2 credits depending on the length of context. Regenerating costs one more credit. Your remaining budget is visible in the composer.
