## Overview

Deal stages define the steps in your sales process. Each stage represents a milestone that a deal passes through on its way to a final outcome. NuCRM lets you fully customize stages, assign win probabilities for forecasting, and configure automated actions that fire when deals enter or leave specific stages.

## Default pipeline stages

NuCRM provides a starting pipeline with these stages:

| Stage | Win probability | Description |
|-------|----------------|-------------|
| Lead | 10% | Initial interest identified |
| Qualified | 25% | Budget, authority, need, timeline confirmed |
| Proposal | 50% | Quote or proposal delivered |
| Negotiation | 75% | Terms being discussed |
| Closed Won | 100% | Deal successfully closed |
| Closed Lost | 0% | Deal did not close |

## Customizing stages

### Accessing stage settings

1. Go to **Settings > Deals > Pipelines**
2. Click the pipeline you want to edit
3. You see the list of stages with their current configuration

### Adding a new stage

1. Click **Add stage**
2. Enter the stage name
3. Set the win probability (used for weighted pipeline forecasting)
4. Choose the position (drag to reorder)
5. Pick a color for the stage indicator
6. Click **Save**

### Editing an existing stage

1. Click the pencil icon next to any stage
2. Modify:
   - **Name** - What the stage is called
   - **Win probability** - Percentage likelihood of closing
   - **Color** - Visual indicator on the board
   - **Description** - Internal note explaining stage criteria
3. Click **Save**

### Removing a stage

1. Click the trash icon next to the stage
2. Choose what happens to deals currently in this stage:
   - Move to a different stage
   - Leave them (they will show in an "unassigned" state until moved)
3. Click **Confirm**

<Callout variant="warning">
Removing a stage affects historical reporting. Deals that were once in that stage will show "Archived stage" in their timeline. Consider renaming the stage instead of deleting if it has historical data.
</Callout>

## Win probabilities and forecasting

### How probability works

Each stage has a win probability percentage that represents the historical likelihood of a deal at that stage eventually closing successfully:

- **Lead (10%)** - Only 1 in 10 leads typically converts
- **Qualified (25%)** - One-quarter of qualified deals close
- **Proposal (50%)** - Half of deals with proposals out will close
- **Negotiation (75%)** - Three-quarters of negotiating deals close

### Weighted pipeline value

The weighted pipeline multiplies each deal's value by its stage probability:

- Deal A: $50,000 in Qualified (25%) = $12,500 weighted
- Deal B: $30,000 in Negotiation (75%) = $22,500 weighted
- Total weighted pipeline: $35,000

This gives a more realistic revenue forecast than the raw pipeline total.

<Callout variant="tip">
Review and adjust your win probabilities quarterly based on actual close rates. If your Proposal stage historically converts at 65%, update the probability from 50% to 65% for more accurate forecasting.
</Callout>

## Stage requirements

Define what must happen before a deal can advance to the next stage:

### Setting requirements

1. Open stage settings for any stage
2. Scroll to **Entry requirements**
3. Add conditions that must be true:
   - Contact must have a phone number
   - Deal must have a close date set
   - At least one note must be logged
   - A specific custom field must be filled
4. Click **Save**

### Enforcement behavior

When requirements are set:

- **Soft enforcement** - Shows a warning when requirements are unmet but allows the move
- **Hard enforcement** - Blocks the stage transition until requirements are satisfied

Choose the enforcement level per requirement.

<Callout variant="note">
Stage requirements help maintain data quality. If your team consistently skips important qualification steps, hard requirements ensure deals cannot progress until those steps are completed.
</Callout>

## Stage automation

Configure actions that trigger automatically when a deal enters a stage:

### Common stage automations

| When deal enters... | Automatic action |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| Qualified | Create task: "Schedule discovery call" |
| Proposal | Send email notification to sales manager |
| Negotiation | Change deal priority to High |
| Closed Won | Create task: "Send welcome package" |
| Closed Won | Update contact lifecycle stage to Customer |
| Closed Lost | Create task: "Send loss survey" |

### Setting up stage automation

1. Open the stage in Settings
2. Scroll to **Automation**
3. Click **Add action**
4. Select the action type:
   - Create task
   - Send notification
   - Update field
   - Send email
   - Add tag
   - Change lifecycle stage
5. Configure the action details
6. Click **Save**

## Stage ordering and grouping

### Open stages vs. closed stages

Stages are categorized as:

- **Open** - Deals are active and in progress (Lead, Qualified, Proposal, Negotiation)
- **Closed Won** - Deal was successful (only one stage of this type per pipeline)
- **Closed Lost** - Deal was unsuccessful (only one stage of this type per pipeline)

This categorization determines:
- Which deals appear in the "open pipeline" value
- How win/loss rates are calculated
- When deals stop appearing on the active board

### Reordering stages

Drag and drop stages in the settings list to change their left-to-right order on the kanban board. The order should reflect your actual sales process sequence.

## Pipeline templates

NuCRM offers pre-built pipeline templates for common business types:

| Template | Stages |
|----------|--------|
| B2B Sales | Lead > Qualified > Demo > Proposal > Negotiation > Closed |
| Real Estate | Inquiry > Viewing > Offer > Under Contract > Closed |
| Recruitment | Applied > Screened > Interview > Offer > Hired |
| SaaS Sales | Trial > Qualified > POC > Procurement > Closed |

Apply a template when creating a new pipeline or use it as a starting point and customize from there.

## Best practices

1. **Keep stages between 4 and 7** - Too few gives no visibility; too many creates confusion
2. **Define clear exit criteria** - Document what must be true for a deal to move forward
3. **Match your actual process** - Stages should reflect reality, not an ideal that nobody follows
4. **Separate pipelines for different processes** - Do not force fundamentally different sales cycles into one pipeline
5. **Review monthly** - Check where deals get stuck and whether stages need splitting or merging
