## What SLA policies control

An SLA (Service Level Agreement) policy defines the maximum time allowed for:

- **First response** — how quickly an agent must acknowledge the ticket
- **Resolution** — how quickly the ticket must be resolved

Different priorities can have different targets.

## Setting up SLA policies

Go to **Settings > SLA** and create a policy:

| Priority | First response target | Resolution target |
|---|---|---|
| Urgent | 15 minutes | 4 hours |
| High | 1 hour | 8 hours |
| Normal | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Low | 8 hours | 48 hours |

Define business hours (e.g. Monday–Friday 9am–6pm) so SLA timers pause outside working hours.

## SLA indicators

On every ticket, a visual indicator shows:

- **Green** — within target, plenty of time remaining
- **Amber** — approaching the target (configurable threshold, e.g. 75% of time elapsed)
- **Red** — breached

The ticket list and kanban views both show these indicators, so breaches are visible at a glance without opening each ticket.

## Breach alerts

When a ticket breaches its SLA:

- The assigned agent receives an immediate notification
- The ticket is flagged in the queue with a red SLA badge
- Optionally, an automation can trigger: escalate to manager, reassign, notify a channel

<Callout variant="warning">
SLA breaches are recorded permanently on the ticket. Even if you respond immediately after a breach, the historical record shows it was late. This matters for reporting and customer reviews.
</Callout>

## SLA reporting

View SLA compliance across your team from **Reports > Support**:

- Compliance rate by agent, team and period
- Average first-response and resolution times
- Breach frequency and patterns (time of day, priority, requester)
