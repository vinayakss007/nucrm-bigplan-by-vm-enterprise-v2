## Overview

Lifecycle stages represent where a contact sits in your overall customer journey, from first awareness through to long-term loyalty. Unlike deal stages (which track individual sales opportunities), lifecycle stages describe the relationship status between your organization and a person. NuCRM provides default stages and allows you to customize them to match your business model.

## Default lifecycle stages

NuCRM includes these stages out of the box:

| Stage | Description | Typical contacts |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Subscriber | Opted in to receive content | Newsletter signups, blog readers |
| Lead | Showed interest but not yet qualified | Form fillers, demo requesters |
| Marketing Qualified (MQL) | Met marketing criteria for sales readiness | Scored leads, engaged prospects |
| Sales Qualified (SQL) | Accepted by sales as a viable opportunity | Contacts with a scheduled meeting |
| Opportunity | Linked to an active deal | Contacts in active negotiations |
| Customer | Purchased your product or service | Closed-won deal contacts |
| Evangelist | Highly satisfied, refers others | Promoters, referral sources |

## Viewing lifecycle stages

### On individual contacts

Each contact displays their lifecycle stage as a colored badge in the profile header:

- **Subscriber** - Gray
- **Lead** - Blue
- **MQL** - Orange
- **SQL** - Purple
- **Opportunity** - Yellow
- **Customer** - Green
- **Evangelist** - Gold

### In list views

The Lifecycle stage column shows the stage badge for each contact. Filter by stage to view all contacts in a particular phase.

### In reporting

The lifecycle funnel report shows:

- Contact count in each stage
- Conversion rate between stages
- Average time spent in each stage
- Trends over time

## Manually changing stages

1. Open the contact detail page
2. Click the lifecycle stage badge in the header
3. Select the new stage from the dropdown
4. The change is saved immediately and logged in the timeline

<Callout variant="note">
Lifecycle stages should generally progress forward (Lead to MQL to SQL). Moving backwards is allowed but is tracked separately in reporting. A "stage regression" entry appears in the timeline when a contact moves to an earlier stage.
</Callout>

## Automatic stage transitions

Configure rules that automatically advance contacts through stages when certain conditions are met.

### Setting up automation rules

1. Go to **Settings > Contacts > Lifecycle stages**
2. Click **Automation rules**
3. Click **Add rule**
4. Define the trigger condition and the target stage

### Common automation examples

| Trigger | New stage |
|---------|-----------|
| Lead score exceeds 70 | Marketing Qualified (MQL) |
| Meeting is scheduled | Sales Qualified (SQL) |
| Deal is created and linked | Opportunity |
| Deal is closed won | Customer |
| Contact submits a referral | Evangelist |

### Rule configuration

Each automation rule includes:

1. **Trigger event** - What action or data change causes the transition
2. **Conditions** - Additional criteria that must be true (optional)
3. **Target stage** - Which stage the contact moves to
4. **Notification** - Optionally notify the contact owner when a transition occurs

<Callout variant="tip">
Start with the most clear-cut transitions (deal closed = Customer) and add more nuanced rules gradually. Over-automating lifecycle stages before you understand your funnel can create confusion.
</Callout>

## Customizing stages

### Adding a custom stage

1. Go to **Settings > Contacts > Lifecycle stages**
2. Click **Add stage**
3. Enter the stage name, color, and position in the sequence
4. Click **Save**

### Editing a stage

1. Click the edit icon next to any stage
2. Modify the name, color, or position
3. Click **Save**

### Removing a stage

1. Click the trash icon next to the stage
2. Choose what happens to contacts currently in this stage:
   - Move them to a different stage
   - Leave them unassigned (not recommended)
3. Confirm deletion

<Callout variant="warning">
Removing a lifecycle stage affects reporting history. Historical data for that stage in funnel reports will show as "Archived stage." Consider renaming or merging instead of deleting.
</Callout>

## Stage-based workflows

Lifecycle stages connect to other NuCRM features:

### Segment by stage

Create segments filtered by lifecycle stage to group contacts for targeted communication:

- "All MQLs created this month"
- "Customers with no support ticket in 90 days"
- "SQLs not yet linked to a deal"

### Stage-triggered automation

Build workflows that fire when a contact enters or leaves a specific stage:

- Send a welcome email when someone becomes a Customer
- Assign a sales rep when a lead reaches MQL
- Create a follow-up task when an Opportunity stalls for 14 days

### Stage-based reporting

Track funnel performance:

- Conversion rates between stages
- Time-to-convert from Lead to Customer
- Drop-off points where contacts fail to advance
- Team performance by stage throughput

## Best practices

1. **Keep stages simple** - Five to seven stages covers most business models
2. **Define clear criteria** - Document what qualifies a contact for each stage so your team is consistent
3. **Automate where possible** - Manual stage management does not scale beyond a few hundred contacts
4. **Review monthly** - Check for stage bottlenecks where contacts accumulate without advancing
